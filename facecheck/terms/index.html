Part of the written reading is generated by Apple's on-device language model, where the device supports it. Two things follow from this that you should know:

  • The model never sees your photograph. It receives only the numeric measurements the App has already computed, and writes prose about them. This is a design constraint of the App, not a preference.
  • Generated text can be wrong, repetitive or oddly phrased. Automated output of this kind is probabilistic by nature. We do not warrant that it is accurate, insightful or appropriate to your situation, and you should treat it as a prompt for reflection rather than a statement of fact.

Where the device does not support the on-device model, or the model declines to respond, the App falls back to text composed from the same measurements. The result is a valid reading either way, and no feature is withheld because of it.

Reviewer notes (remove before publication)

Verified against the source code on August 26, 2026. Each commercial and functional claim above traces to a specific finding:

  • Two non-consumable products, no subscription: Tier declares exactly quickread and complete in FaceCheck/Store/Store.swift:5-10; Products.storekit lists both as NonConsumable at 4.99 and 6.99, with no subscription group.
  • What each tier unlocks: five cards gate on hasQuickAccess and one, the facial signature, on hasFullAccess (FaceCheck/Views/ResultsView.swift:192-196). Section 8 lists them exactly as gated.
  • Apple verifies entitlements, we do not: Transaction.currentEntitlements and AppStore.sync() in Store.swift:56-75. There is no receipt-validation server.
  • Revocation relocks features: refreshEntitlements() skips transactions with a non-nil revocationDate (Store.swift:58), which is what section 8's last-but-one paragraph describes.
  • No accounts, no termination mechanism: no authentication, no user record and no remote kill switch exists anywhere in the source. Section 15 is written to match, rather than claiming a suspension right the App cannot exercise.
  • No networking: the only URL in the entire app is the App Store review link (FaceCheck/Settings/AppInfo.swift:23). No URLSession, no analytics, no tracking.
  • No third-party dependencies: project.yml declares no packages; only Apple frameworks are imported.
  • Photos are never transmitted. Since August 27, 2026 the App does persist a reading history on device: a 200×200 thumbnail plus the numeric result per reading, capped at 100 and excluded from backups. Section 5 was rewritten for this; the full-size image is still never written to disk.
  • The language model never sees the photo: CopyWriter passes computed measurements as text only, with a non-AI fallback path (FaceCheck/Analysis/CopyWriter.swift:33-162). Section 6 states this as a design constraint because it is one.
  • Analysis can fail: FaceAnalysisError.noFaceDetected and .noLandmarks (FaceCheck/Analysis/FaceAnalyzer.swift:31-62) support the last paragraph of section 4.
  • Sharing is user-initiated: a ShareLink over a rendered card (ResultsView.swift:122), never automatic.
  • iOS 18 minimum: project.yml:4-5.
  • Scores are provisional: the scoring engine documents its anchors and percentile distribution as v0 pending calibration (FaceCheck/Analysis/ScoreEngine.swift:56-58), which is why section 4 disclaims comparability between versions.

Rights deliberately not asserted: no licence to your photos (we never receive them), no account-suspension right (no accounts exist), no right to disable the App remotely (no such mechanism), and no promise of future features beyond the honest caveat in section 8.
