Tile Match vs. Mahjong Solitaire: What's the Difference?

By Nicolas Gula · Updated 2026-10-12 · Trip Sort for iPhone and Android

Mahjong solitaire and mahjong tile match are cousins, not twins. In classic mahjong solitaire you remove matching pairs of "free" tiles straight from a stacked layout; in a triple tile match game you tap tiles into a small tray and clear them in sets of three. Both reward the same habit of reading the layers, but the tray changes how you plan every move.

How classic mahjong solitaire works

Mahjong solitaire borrows the tiles of the traditional four-player game of mahjong, but none of its rules. It first appeared as a computer game in the early 1980s and has been one of the most popular forms of tile solitaire ever since. The classic version works like this:

  • The set. A full set has 144 tiles: four copies each of 34 standard tiles (the dots, bamboo and characters suits, plus the winds and dragons), and eight bonus tiles, four flowers and four seasons.
  • The layout. All the tiles are stacked face up in a layered shape. The best known is the pyramid-like "turtle", but there are hundreds of layouts.
  • Free tiles. A tile is free when no tile sits on top of it, even partly, and at least one of its long sides, left or right, is open. A tile wedged between neighbors on both sides is blocked.
  • Pairs. You remove two identical free tiles at a time. The bonus tiles are the exception: any flower matches any flower, and any season matches any season.
  • Winning and losing. You win when the layout is empty. You are stuck when no pair of free tiles is left.

The deep part of mahjong solitaire is choosing which pair to take when several are available. Because there are four copies of each tile, taking the wrong two can bury the last pair under each other, and you only find out many moves later.

How a mahjong tile match game works

"Mahjong tile match" is what many players call the newer triple tile match style, because the board looks a lot like a mahjong layout: pictures on tiles, stacked in overlapping layers. The rules, though, are different:

  1. You tap one tile at a time, and it moves off the board into a tray.
  2. When three identical tiles are in the tray, they clear.
  3. You can usually pick any tile that is not covered by another one; there is no left-or-right rule.
  4. If the tray fills up without a match, the level is lost.

Mahjong solitaire vs. tile match: the key differences

  • Pairs vs. triples. Mahjong solitaire clears two tiles per move. Tile match needs three, so you often hold one or two tiles while you look for the rest.
  • Free tiles vs. uncovered tiles. In mahjong solitaire a tile also needs an open long side. In tile match, being uncovered is usually enough, so more tiles are playable at once.
  • No tray vs. a tray. Mahjong solitaire has no holding area: a pair comes off together or not at all. In tile match, the tray is your limited memory, and managing it is the heart of the strategy.
  • How you get stuck. In mahjong solitaire you run out of free pairs. In tile match you fill the tray with tiles that cannot complete a set.
  • The tiles themselves. Mahjong solitaire uses Chinese suits, winds and dragons, which take a little learning. Tile match games usually use everyday pictures you recognize instantly.

Why fans of mahjong style games feel at home in tile match

If you love mahjong style games, most of your instincts carry over. You already scan layered boards, notice which tiles are trapped under others and think about what each move uncovers. In mahjong solitaire, you learn not to take a pair if it buries the other two copies; in tile match, you learn not to take a tile if it fills the tray without getting you closer to a set. Same idea, different pressure.

The main adjustment is the tray. A few habits make the switch easier:

  1. Before tapping, count how many copies of a tile you can reach right now.
  2. Prefer finishing a set already in the tray over starting a new one.
  3. Use early moves to uncover the layers, just as you would open up a blocked row in mahjong solitaire.

Questions people also ask

Is tile match the same as mahjong?

No. Traditional mahjong is a four-player game with its own scoring. Mahjong solitaire uses the same tiles to remove pairs of free tiles from a layout. Tile match borrows the layered look but clears tiles in sets of three through a small tray.

What makes a tile free in mahjong solitaire?

A tile is free when no other tile sits on top of it and at least one of its long sides, left or right, is not touching another tile. Only free tiles can be matched and removed.

Is tile match easier than mahjong solitaire?

It is easier to learn, because the pictures are familiar and the only rule is to collect three of a kind. It is not always easier to win: the small tray means a few careless taps can end a level, so planning matters just as much.
