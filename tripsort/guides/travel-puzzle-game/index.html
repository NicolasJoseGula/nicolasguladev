A Travel Puzzle Game: Visit 255 Destinations From Your Phone

Updated 2026-10-12 · Trip Sort for iPhone and Android

A travel puzzle game wraps short puzzle levels inside a trip, so every board you clear moves you a little further along a route through real places. In Trip Sort that trip covers 255 destinations on every continent: you match city-themed tiles, fill in a postcard for each stop, stamp your passport and collect famous landmarks along the way.

What makes a good travel puzzle game

Plenty of games put a world map on the menu screen and call it a trip. The ones that actually feel like travel tend to share a few traits, and they are worth checking before you download anything:

  • Real places, not generic scenery. Each stop should look and feel like the city it is named after, with its own food, buildings and symbols.
  • A sense of distance. Moving from one city to the next should feel like a journey: a route on a map, a plane or a train, a new place at the end.
  • Something to keep. Souvenirs, stamps or collections give you a record of where you have been, the same way photos do on a real trip.
  • Puzzles that stand on their own. The theme is the reward, but the levels still need to be fun to solve. A good puzzle with a pretty backdrop beats a pretty backdrop with a weak puzzle.
  • Playable anywhere. If the theme is travel, the game should work where travellers are: on planes, trains and in places with no signal.

How a world tour game turns levels into a trip

The puzzle at the heart of Trip Sort is a triple tile match: tap a tile and it moves into a tray with 4 slots, and three identical tiles in the tray clear. If you are new to the genre, our guide on what a triple tile match game is covers the rules in detail. What turns those boards into a world tour game is how they connect:

  1. Every city has 12 levels. Each one is a board built from that city's own tiles.
  2. Every level adds a piece to the postcard. As you clear boards, the city's postcard fills in bit by bit, so you can see how close you are to finishing the stop.
  3. Finishing a city stamps your passport. The destination gets a "VISITED" stamp, a small moment that feels a lot like the real thing.
  4. Then you travel. You move on to the next stop by plane, ship, train or bus, with an animation across the map. If you are in a hurry, tap to skip it.

255 destinations: a travel the world game on every continent

The trip starts in Montevideo and covers 255 real destinations on every continent. With 12 levels per stop, that adds up to over 3,000 levels, which is a long journey even if you play every day. What keeps it from feeling repetitive is that each city brings its own tiles, so the board itself tells you where you are:

  • Paris: the Eiffel Tower, croissants and macarons.
  • Kyoto: torii gates and sushi.
  • Rio de Janeiro: Christ the Redeemer and toucans.
  • Buenos Aires: tango shoes and mate.
  • London: Big Ben and the double-decker bus.

Spotting a familiar dish or building is part of the fun, and the less familiar ones are a nice nudge to look a place up. The difficulty grows with the trip too: early boards are calm and easy, and later boards stack tiles in layers and ask you to think a few moves ahead. There are no timers at any point, so you set the pace.

Collecting famous landmarks along the way

On top of postcards and stamps, there are 23 famous landmarks to collect. You earn each one by completing the destination where it stands, and each comes with a short real-world story, so your collection doubles as a small travel guide. The list includes the Eiffel Tower, the Colosseum, the Taj Mahal, Chichen Itza, the Great Wall of China, the Sydney Opera House, the Treasury of Petra, Angkor Wat, Uluru and Mount Fuji, among others.

A few practical ideas if you enjoy the collecting side:

  • Read each landmark's story when you unlock it. It takes a minute and makes the next city feel less like just another board.
  • Use the postcard as a progress bar. If you only have five minutes, finishing one more piece of the postcard is a good small goal.
  • Replay a favourite city. Open the Journey, tap a destination you completed and pick any of its 12 levels.

A vacation game that works on real trips

A travel theme is nice at home, but a good vacation game should also earn its place on an actual trip. Long flights, train rides, airport gates and hotel rooms with weak Wi-Fi are exactly where you want something light to do. Trip Sort plays every level with no internet: offline there are simply no ads and the Shop is not available. There is no account or sign-up either, and progress is saved on the device. If you are packing for a flight, our guide to games to play on a plane has a short checklist for getting your phone ready.

The game is also translated into 70+ languages, which helps if you share a phone or tablet with family on the road.

How to do it in Trip Sort

  1. Download Trip Sort on iPhone or Android. It is free, with no account to create.
  2. Start your trip in Montevideo and clear the first board: tap tiles to move them into the tray and match three of a kind.
  3. Watch the city's postcard fill in with each level, and finish all 12 to get your "VISITED" passport stamp.
  4. Enjoy the plane, ship, train or bus ride to the next stop, or tap to skip it.
  5. Complete destinations with famous landmarks to add them to your collection, and read the story that comes with each one.
  6. To revisit a city, open the Journey, tap a completed destination and pick any of its levels.

Questions people also ask

What is a travel puzzle game?

It is a puzzle game where the levels are organized as a trip through real places. In Trip Sort, each of the 255 destinations has 12 tile-matching levels with its own city tiles, a postcard to complete and a passport stamp when you finish.

How many destinations and levels does Trip Sort have?

Trip Sort has 255 real destinations on every continent, with 12 levels each, for over 3,000 levels in total. The trip starts in Montevideo.

Can I play a travel puzzle game offline?

Yes. Every Trip Sort level plays with no internet, so it works on planes, trains and anywhere without signal. Offline there are no ads and the Shop is not available.
