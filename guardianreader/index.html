Inside the App

Block apps until the reading is done.

A parental control and a reading coach in one. Your child runs it alone; the iPhone does the checking.

Live verification

Watch it verify, word by word.

While your child reads out loud, the app matches what it hears against the scanned pages and the percentage climbs live. This is a real session, reading a paper book to an iPhone. Honest reading lands far above the pass mark; talking about something else lands near zero.

Reading aloud is the exercise →

Scan the pages

Any paper book becomes the key.

Your child photographs every page they are going to read, one photo per page, then reads them all straight through like a real book. Only the text inside the framing guide counts, and a page already scanned in that session is rejected automatically.

Anti-cheating

It catches the shortcuts kids actually take.

No quiz to guess, no honor system. The spoken words are compared against the exact pages they scanned, in order, so skimming, improvising or claiming it is done does not pass. Stumbles and small mistakes do: it checks that they read, not that they were perfect. A slider lets you set how strict the pass mark is.

How earning screen time works →

The reward

Pass the reading. Unlock the phone.

The apps open for exactly the minutes they earned, with a countdown in plain sight. When it hits zero they lock again by themselves, enforced by Apple's Screen Time system, even if Guardian Reader is closed.

Parent rules

You set the exchange rate.

Pages in, minutes out: one page earns about 15 minutes at ages 4 to 6, up to four pages for about 45 minutes for teens. The plan suggests a starting point by age, and every number can be changed behind a parent PIN that is separate from the phone passcode.

Progress & streaks

Medals that keep them coming back.

Streaks, stars and medals for the child; pages per day, month and year for you. Premium adds a PDF report of every session: date, minutes, match percentage and the text they actually read, ready to hand to a teacher.

Private by design

A microphone pointed at a child should explain itself.

The camera reads the page, the microphone transcribes the reading, and both work on the device only. No account, no server, no analytics, no third-party SDKs: the voice never leaves the iPhone, and everything works offline.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How does Guardian Reader verify my child actually read the book?
Your child photographs every page they are going to read, one photo per page. The app extracts the text from those photos, then listens while they read it all out loud, straight through, and compares what it hears against that text, word by word. Reading honestly scores far above the pass mark; talking about something else scores near zero. All of it happens on the iPhone. Learn more
Can my child cheat by pretending to read?
Not by pretending. The app compares the spoken words against the exact pages they scanned, in order, so improvising, mumbling or skipping ahead does not match the text, and a page already scanned in the same session cannot count twice. What it verifies is that those words were read out loud, not who said them: there is no voice recognition. See the next question. Learn more
Could an AI voice read the page instead of my child?
Yes, and we would rather say so. Guardian Reader compares what the microphone hears against the scanned page; it does not identify who is speaking. A synthetic voice reading that same text on a second device would pass, and so would an older sibling. What the app reliably stops is the everyday version: skimming, improvising, or saying it is done. A child willing to photograph a page, retype it into a voice generator and play it back has a problem the app was never going to solve.
Do I install Guardian Reader on my phone or my child's?
On your child's iPhone, the one with the apps you want to lock. There is no remote dashboard on your phone: you configure everything on their device, and the settings stay behind a parent PIN only you know. Family Sharing is not required. Learn more
Which apps can I block on my child's iPhone?
Any app or whole category installed on the iPhone, using Apple's Screen Time framework: YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, games, or every social app at once. Free blocks one app; Premium blocks as many as you need. Learn more
Does Guardian Reader record my child's voice?
No. The microphone is used to transcribe the reading on the device while the session is running. There is no account, no server and no upload: the voice never leaves the iPhone, and nothing is tracked, sold or shared. Learn more
What books work with the app?
Any printed book, magazine or comic with readable text. The child photographs one page at a time with a framing guide, so only the text inside the frame counts. It works in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean and Chinese. Learn more
What age is Guardian Reader for?
Roughly ages 4 to 16. During setup you pick an age band and the app adjusts the pages per session and how strict the match has to be, so a child learning to read is not held to the same standard as a teenager. Learn more
How much does Guardian Reader cost?
Free: verified reading aloud, screen time rewards, streaks, medals and the last 30 days of history, with one blocked app. Premium: unlimited blocked apps, the full history and PDF reading reports, with a 7 day free trial on the yearly plan ($19.99/year) or a one-time $49.99 lifetime option.
Can I get a reading log for school?
Yes. Premium exports a PDF with every session: date, time, pages, minutes, match percentage and the text your child actually read aloud. It is ready to hand to a teacher as evidence of at-home reading practice. Learn more
Does it work without internet?
Yes. Reading the pages, verifying them and unlocking screen time all work offline, because the text recognition and the speech transcription run on the iPhone itself. Learn more
Can my child just uninstall the app or change the settings?
Settings are behind a parent PIN that is separate from the phone passcode and from Face ID, so the child cannot open them. The blocking itself is enforced by Apple's Screen Time system, and when the earned minutes run out the apps lock again automatically, even if Guardian Reader is closed. Learn more
