Word-by-word karaoke captions

Updated 2026-08-07 · Charlatan for iPhone

Karaoke captions show a short phrase and light up each word at the exact moment it is spoken. It is the single most recognisable caption style in short-form video, and unlike most trends it has a reason behind it.

Here is what it does, when to use it, and when it is the wrong choice.

Why it holds attention

A static caption gives the eye nothing to do. It lands, you read it in half a second, and then you are waiting, and waiting is where people swipe.

A moving highlight keeps the eye tracking left to right in time with the voice. Reading and listening stay synchronised instead of drifting, and there is never a moment where the screen has nothing happening on it.

The two ways to do it

  • Highlight: the whole phrase is on screen and the current word changes colour or gets a block behind it. Easier to read, because the viewer can see what is coming.
  • Reveal: words appear one at a time. More dramatic, more aggressive, and harder to follow if the person is speaking quickly.
  • For most talking-head content, highlight beats reveal. Save reveal for punchlines and single-sentence hooks.

When not to use it

Karaoke captions work because they match the pace of speech. If your content is dense (numbers, names, instructions someone might need to pause on) a full sentence they can read at their own pace serves them better.

They are also wrong for anything long. Over a couple of minutes the constant movement stops being energy and starts being noise.

How to do it in Charlatan

  1. Open the clip in Charlatan and let it transcribe. Word timings come from the transcription, so there is nothing to line up by hand.
  2. Set the pace to Punch for two to three word phrases: the sweet spot for this style.
  3. Turn on karaoke highlight in the Style panel.
  4. Pick the highlight colour. High contrast against your footage is what matters: cyan, yellow and green all read strongly over skin tones and interiors.
  5. Preview it full screen before exporting. Karaoke timing is the one thing that looks wrong immediately if it is off, and right immediately if it is not.

Questions people also ask

What are karaoke captions called?

Word-by-word captions, karaoke captions and highlight captions all describe the same thing: a caption where the current word is emphasised as it is spoken. Some editors call it word-level highlighting.

Do I have to time each word by hand?

No. The timing comes out of the speech recognition, which already knows when each word was said. Hand-timing is only necessary in editors that have no transcription step.
