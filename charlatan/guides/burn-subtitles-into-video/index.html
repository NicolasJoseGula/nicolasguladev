How to burn subtitles into a video

Updated 2026-08-07 · Charlatan for iPhone

"Burned in" and "hardcoded" mean the same thing: the text is drawn into the video frames themselves, so it is as much a part of the picture as anything that was in front of the camera.

The alternative is a separate subtitle file, SRT or VTT, that a player reads alongside the video. Both are correct in different places.

When burned-in is right

  • Social video, always. TikTok, Reels and Shorts have no way to carry your subtitle file, and the audience watches muted by default.
  • Anything you will repost across several platforms and want to look identical on each.
  • Any style you actually care about: colour, outline, custom font, karaoke highlight. None of that survives in an SRT, which carries text and timings and nothing else.
  • Anything sent as a plain file (WhatsApp, email, a shared album) where nobody is going to attach a second file.

When a separate file is right

  • Long-form video on YouTube or a website, where viewers may want to turn captions off, change the language, or use a screen reader.
  • Anything that needs to be searchable: a transcript file is text a search engine can read, and pixels are not.
  • Broadcast, streaming platforms and accessibility compliance, which usually require selectable captions specifically.
  • Anything you will need to correct later without re-rendering the video.

The honest downside

Burned-in captions cannot be turned off or edited after export. If a word is wrong, you re-export.

In practice this is smaller than it sounds, because you keep the project and re-exporting is a couple of taps. It is worth naming, though, rather than pretending the choice is free.

How to do it in Charlatan

  1. Open the clip in Charlatan. The transcription runs on the phone and gives you an editable caption list.
  2. Fix any misheard word in the Text panel before you style anything: it is the one step that is cheaper to do now than later.
  3. Choose the font, colour, outline and pace.
  4. Pick your aspect ratio: 9:16, 1:1, 16:9 or the original framing.
  5. Export. Charlatan renders the captions into every frame, so the file you get is the file everyone sees.

Questions people also ask

What does hardcoded subtitles mean?

It means the subtitle text has been rendered into the video's pixels rather than stored separately. Hardcoded, burned-in and open captions all describe the same result: text that cannot be switched off because it is part of the image.

Can I burn subtitles into a video on my phone?

Yes. The whole process (transcribing, styling and rendering the captions into the frames) runs on a modern iPhone without a computer or an upload.
