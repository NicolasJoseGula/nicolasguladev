How to add subtitles to YouTube Shorts

By Nicolas Gula · Updated 2026-08-07 · Charlatan for iPhone

YouTube generates automatic captions for Shorts, but they are closed captions: off by default for many viewers, styled by YouTube, and shown in a fixed position you do not control.

For a format that autoplays muted, that is not the same thing as having captions.

Closed captions are opt-in, burned captions are not

YouTube's automatic captions have to be turned on. A large share of viewers never touch that setting, so for them your Short plays silent and wordless.

Burned-in captions do not ask. They are part of the picture, so the first second of your video communicates whether or not anyone has changed a setting.

The Shorts safe zone

  • Bottom ~15%: title, channel name and the Shorts remix bar. Text here is covered.
  • Right ~12%: like, dislike, comment, share and the sound icon.
  • Top: occasionally a header, plus the device status bar.
  • Aim for the middle to lower-middle of the frame, centred, and check it full screen rather than trusting a small preview.

Do both

Burned captions are for the viewer. YouTube's automatic transcript is for search: YouTube reads it to understand what your Short is about, and it powers accessibility features that burned text cannot.

So burn your styled captions into the file and leave YouTube's automatic captions on. They serve different jobs and they do not conflict.

How to do it in Charlatan

  1. Import or record the clip in Charlatan and let it transcribe on the device.
  2. Select 9:16 so the frame matches what Shorts will display.
  3. Style the captions and drag them clear of the bottom 15% and the right-hand button strip.
  4. Use the fullscreen preview to confirm nothing collides with where the interface will be.
  5. Export and upload. Leave YouTube's own automatic captions enabled for search and accessibility.

Questions people also ask

Does YouTube add subtitles to Shorts automatically?

It generates automatic closed captions from the audio, but viewers have to turn them on, and you cannot change how they look or where they sit. Burned-in captions are always visible and always styled the way you chose.

What size should a YouTube Short be?

1080 x 1920, which is 9:16 vertical, up to three minutes. Exporting at that ratio means YouTube has nothing to crop, which is what usually clips captions placed near an edge.
