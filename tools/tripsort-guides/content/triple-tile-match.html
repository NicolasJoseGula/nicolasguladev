A triple tile match game is a puzzle where you tap tiles on a board to send them into a small tray, and any three identical tiles in the tray disappear. You win by clearing every tile from the board before the tray fills up with tiles that don't match.

That is the whole rulebook. What makes the genre interesting is what it hides: which tiles you can actually reach, how little room the tray gives you, and how a simple-looking board can turn tricky three moves later. Here are the rules, the ideas that matter most and how to clear your first boards.

How a triple tile match game works

Almost every tile match game follows the same loop. Once you know it, you can pick up any game in the genre in a minute.

  1. Look at the board. Tiles are scattered across it, often stacked in layers, and each one shows a picture.
  2. Tap a free tile. A tile is free when nothing is lying on top of it. When you tap it, it leaves the board and slides into the tray.
  3. Collect three of a kind. When three identical tiles land in the tray, they clear and free up their slots.
  4. Keep going until the board is empty. Clearing the last tile wins the level.
  5. Watch the tray. If it fills up and no three tiles in it match, the level is lost.

The tray: the part beginners underestimate

The tray is the heart of any triple tile match game. It is a short row of slots next to the board (in Trip Sort it sits just above it), and it is the only place where matches happen. Its size changes from game to game. In Trip Sort the tray has four slots, which means you are always only a few taps away from trouble, and every tap deserves a quick thought.

Think of the tray as a tiny desk. Every tile you put on it takes up space until it finds its two partners. If you pick up tiles that have no partners nearby, the desk fills with clutter and there is nowhere left for the tile you actually need. A few rules of thumb help:

  • Before you tap, find all three copies of a tile, or at least know where the third one is hiding.
  • Try to finish a set before you start a new one. Two unfinished pairs already fill a four-slot tray.
  • Treat the last empty slot as an emergency exit. Only fill it when the tile completes a match.

Covered tiles and layers: what you can pick

On a flat board every tile is free, so the game is just a search for matches. Real boards are rarely flat. Tiles sit on top of other tiles, sometimes several layers deep, and only the tiles that are not covered by anything can be picked. Covered tiles usually look darker or partly hidden, which is your signal to leave them for later.

Layers are what turn tile matching into a planning game. The tile you need might be sitting under a tile you don't want, so sometimes the right move is to take a tile you don't need yet just to uncover one you do. That is fine, as long as the tray has room and you know where the partners of that extra tile are.

When you look at a layered board, scan it from the top down: what is free now, what will be free after one tap and which stacks hide the most tiles. If you want to go deeper, our guide on how to beat hard tile match levels covers reading layers in detail.

How to play tile match: your first boards, step by step

The first boards of a good tile match game are gentle on purpose. Use them to build habits that will pay off later, when boards get deeper.

  1. Take a moment before the first tap. Look at the whole board and spot two or three pictures that already have three free copies.
  2. Clear those easy sets first. Each finished set empties part of the tray and often uncovers new tiles.
  3. Then go for the tiles that block big stacks. The more of the board you uncover, the more choices you have.
  4. Check the tray after every tap and ask yourself: if I take this tile, can I still finish what is already in there?
  5. Don't rush. Many tile match games, Trip Sort included, have no timer, so there is no reason to tap fast.

Why match 3 tiles instead of pairs?

If you grew up with mahjong solitaire, you already know the idea of free and covered tiles. The big difference is that mahjong solitaire removes pairs straight from the board, while a triple tile match game makes you collect three in a tray. That tray adds a layer of memory and planning: you are holding tiles while you look for the rest. We compare both in tile match vs. mahjong solitaire.

In a travel-themed game, the pictures themselves become part of the fun: you are not matching abstract symbols but croissants, toucans and famous landmarks.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Tapping the first pair you see. A free pair is useless if the third tile is buried several layers down.
  • Starting too many sets at once. With a small tray, two or three loose tiles are already a lot.
  • Using boosters too early. Help tools do the most good when you are really stuck, not on the first move.

Questions people also ask

Is triple tile match the same as mahjong?

No. Mahjong solitaire matches pairs of free tiles directly on the board. A triple tile match game sends tiles to a tray and clears them in sets of three, so you have to plan around the tray's limited space.

What happens when the tray is full?

If the tray fills up and no three tiles in it match, the level is lost. In Trip Sort you can continue with an optional rewarded video or with coins, or retry the level.
