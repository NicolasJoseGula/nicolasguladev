To beat hard tile match levels, slow down and plan around the tray: uncover hidden tiles before you commit, only start a set when you know where all three tiles are, and keep at least one tray slot free. Save boosters like Undo, Shuffle and the Magic Wand for the moment you are truly stuck, not for the first move.

Hard boards rarely beat you with one big mistake. They beat you with small ones: a tile taken a little too early, a set started without its third copy in sight. If you want to know how to win tile match levels that seem impossible, the nine tips below are about avoiding those small mistakes. They work in almost any triple tile match game, and at the end you will see how to apply them in Trip Sort.

Why hard tile match levels feel so hard

Easy boards are mostly flat, so almost every tile is free and a match is always within reach. Hard boards stack tiles in layers. Most of the board is covered at the start, the copies of each picture are spread across different layers, and the tray is small: in Trip Sort it holds just four tiles. That combination means the order of your taps matters more than how fast you spot matches.

If you are new to the genre, start with the basic rules of triple tile match. The tips below assume you already know how the tray and covered tiles work.

Read the board before you tap (tips 1 to 3)

1. Scan every layer first

Take ten seconds before your first move. Note which tiles are free now, which are only partly covered and which stacks are the tallest. Tiles that peek out from under others tell you what is coming next.

2. Count to three before you commit

Don't start a set unless you can see where all three copies are, or at least know the third one will be free soon. A lonely tile in the tray is a slot you lose until its partners show up.

3. Dig into the tallest stacks early

Tall stacks hide the most tiles, and the tiles at the bottom of a stack are the ones that cost you levels when you can't reach them. Uncovering big stacks early gives you more options later, when the tray is under pressure.

Tile match strategy for the tray (tips 4 to 6)

4. Keep one slot free

Treat the last empty slot as off-limits unless the tile you are placing completes a set. That one free slot is what lets you react when an unexpected tile appears.

5. Finish sets before you start new ones

With a four-slot tray, two unfinished pairs already fill it. Work on one set at a time and close it before you open the next. If you must hold two different tiles, make sure the partners of at least one of them are free right now.

6. Take "useless" tiles on purpose

Sometimes the tile you need is under a tile you don't. It is fine to take that blocking tile, as long as its partners are free or about to be, so it won't sit in the tray for long. Knowing when to make this kind of move is what separates hard boards from easy ones.

Use boosters at the right moment (tips 7 and 8)

Most tile match games give you a few help tools. They are limited, so the question is not whether to use them but when. We cover each one in more depth in tile match boosters explained.

7. Use Undo to scout

Undo puts your last tile back on the board. It is most valuable right after a tap that revealed a bad surprise, like a tile whose partners are buried deep. Take the tile, see what is underneath, and undo if it was a trap.

8. Save the big boosters for real dead ends

  • Shuffle mixes up the tiles left on the board. Use it when none of the free tiles have partners within reach.
  • Magic Wand clears a whole set for you. It is strongest late in a level, when one picture is causing most of your trouble.
  • Extra Slot adds a fifth slot to the tray. Use it when you are one tile away from losing but can see the match coming.

How to beat hard tile match levels when you are really stuck (tip 9)

9. Retry with what you learned

A lost level is not wasted time. You now know which stack caused trouble and which pictures were buried deepest. Retry, and let that shape your first moves. If you still can't see a safe tap, a hint that lights up the best move is often all you need to get going again.

And take a break when you need one. Coming back to a board later with fresh eyes often shows you a move you missed.

Questions people also ask

Is there a trick to winning tile match games?

There is no single trick, but one habit helps most: only start a set when you know where all three tiles are, and keep one tray slot free. Uncover big stacks early and save boosters for real dead ends.

Do tile match levels get harder as you go?

Yes, in most games. In Trip Sort the early boards are calm and easy, and later boards stack tiles in layers that ask you to think a few moves ahead.

Should I use boosters early or save them?

Save them. Boosters are limited, and they do the most good when you are actually stuck. Undo is the exception: use it right away when a tap reveals a bad surprise.
