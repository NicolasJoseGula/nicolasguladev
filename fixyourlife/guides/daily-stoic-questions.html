Daily Stoic Questions: A Simple Morning & Evening Practice

Before stoicism was a bookshelf genre, it was a daily discipline — and its core tool wasn't a quote. It was a question, asked on schedule, answered in writing.

The Stoics were the original daily journalers

Marcus Aurelius ruled an empire, and still ended his days writing private notes to himself — the book we now call Meditations was never meant for readers. Seneca described putting himself on trial each night: examining the whole day and hiding nothing from himself. Epictetus told students to keep their principles "at hand" — procheiron — ready before events demanded them.

Strip away twenty centuries and the practice is startlingly simple: a morning preparation, an evening review, and honest written answers. No incense, no hour of silence. Questions, on a schedule.

Morning questions — prepare the day

  • What is in my control today — and what am I pretending is?
  • What obstacle is likely today, and how will I meet it?
  • If today were my last, would I spend it as I'm about to?
  • What would the person I'm trying to become do before noon?

Evening questions — put the day on trial

  • What did I do well today? Where did I act against my values?
  • What upset me today that was never in my control?
  • What bad habit did I feed today — and what did I curb?
  • What am I avoiding that I know I should face?
The Stoic question isn't meant to be contemplated. It's meant to be answered — briefly, honestly, today.

Why the practice dies without a schedule

Here's what the philosophy books skip: Marcus Aurelius had the discipline of a Roman military camp structuring his hours. You have a phone with forty apps engineered against your attention. Reading stoic quotes between distractions isn't a practice — it's decoration. The practice is the appointment: fixed times when the question arrives and demands an answer, whether you feel philosophical or not.

How to run a stoic practice with Fix Your Life

Fix Your Life turns your phone into the discipline Marcus had:

  • Set two alarms: a morning preparation (e.g., 7:00) and an evening review (e.g., 21:30). They ring until you face them.
  • Write your own stoic questions as custom messages — the ones above, or your own translations of them. Save as many as you like.
  • Turn on Surprise mode to draw a different question from your set each day, like opening Meditations at random.
  • Answer in one line. Your Journal becomes your own private Meditations — dated, searchable, and yours alone. The streak and heatmap keep the discipline honest.
