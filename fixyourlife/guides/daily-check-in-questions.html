Daily Check-In Questions That Keep You on Track

Plans fail quietly, in the middle of ordinary afternoons. A daily check-in is how you catch the drift while there's still time to correct it.

Why check-ins beat planning

Most productivity advice is about planning: the perfect morning routine, the perfect task list. But plans don't fail at the planning stage — they fail at 2:47 PM, when you've been "quickly checking" something for an hour and nobody notices, including you.

A check-in is the opposite of a plan. It doesn't ask what will you do? It asks what are you actually doing, right now? Pilots don't fly a straight line to their destination; they drift constantly and correct constantly. The correction is only possible because they check the instruments on a schedule.

The questions

Morning — set the vector

  • What is the one thing that would make today a win?
  • Are you doing everything necessary to achieve your goals?
  • What will you say no to today?

Midday — catch the drift

  • Is what you're doing right now moving you toward what you want?
  • Are you spending time on things you truly want?
  • If someone filmed the last two hours of your life, what would they conclude you want?

Evening — close the loop

  • What did you do today that your future self will thank you for?
  • What did you avoid today — and why?
  • What would you do differently if tomorrow were a repeat of today?

The three rules that make it work

  1. The question must arrive on its own. If you have to remember to check in, you won't — the whole problem is that drift feels normal from the inside. Put the questions on alarms.
  2. Answer in writing. One line is enough. A written "No, I've been avoiding the hard task all morning" creates accountability a silent shrug never will.
  3. Rotate the questions. Any question you see daily becomes wallpaper within a week. Variety keeps the edge.
You don't need more discipline. You need to notice sooner.

How to run daily check-ins with Fix Your Life

Fix Your Life turns this whole system into three alarms:

  • Create a morning, midday and evening alarm — for example 7:00, 13:00 and 21:30, on the days you choose.
  • Assign questions from the presets or write your own for each slot. Turn on Surprise mode so each alarm pulls a fresh question daily and never goes stale.
  • The alarm rings until you answer — one honest line. No snoozing past the check-in.
  • Review your Journal weekly. Your answers form a drift log: you'll see exactly which hours and days pull you off course.
