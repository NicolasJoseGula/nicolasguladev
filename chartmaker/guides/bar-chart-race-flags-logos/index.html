Flags, crests and logos on the bars

Updated 2026-08-08 · Chart Maker for Mac

Two ways to get a picture onto every bar: a country code, or a column of addresses in your own file.

Country flags: just add a code

Add a column called ISO2 (or Code, or Country Code) holding the two-letter country code: us, fr, jp, and every bar gets its round flag.

265 round flags ship inside the app. They are not fetched from anywhere, which means they work offline, they cannot break because somebody else’s server moved, and they are the same flags in every export you ever make.

Anything else: a column of addresses

For club crests, company logos, player faces or your own artwork, add a column of image addresses. Any of these headings is recognised: Image, Logo, Badge, Crest, Photo, Icon, Flag. A picture of the competitor’s own always wins over the flag its country code would have produced.

How each kind is drawn

A flag is fitted whole, because a round flag is drawn for a round frame and cropping one loses a stripe. A photograph or a crest is filled and clipped to the circle, because a rectangle fitted whole leaves bars down the sides and its corners poke through the ring.

If your artwork is drawn on white, most company wordmarks are, switch the chart to the light theme, or the mark will vanish into a dark ground. The Market Caps template is set up that way already.

When a picture does not arrive

An address that fails leaves an empty badge, and an empty badge cannot tell you why. So the app answers all three possibilities: View data shows which column became the picture and the address read on every row, and the sidebar reports any picture that did not load, with its address. Some image servers refuse requests that do not come from their own site: if a batch never arrives, host them somewhere that allows it.

Questions

Do the flags work offline?

Yes. All 265 ship inside the app, so a flag never depends on a network connection.

Can I use my own logo on the video?

Yes - separately from the bars, there is a logo slot in the top corner of the frame, opposite the title.
