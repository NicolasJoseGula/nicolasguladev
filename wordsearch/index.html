How it works

Find the word, learn the word

Each level takes a couple of minutes, and each word goes through the four steps that make vocabulary stick.

See the picture

Each level shows picture stickers: a lion, an apple, the rain. That is the word you look for.

Find the word

Swipe the English word in the letter grid. Words run across, down and diagonally.

Hear it, read it

The moment you find it you hear the pronunciation and see the translation in your language.

Meet it again

Words come back in later levels, and soon you only get the first letter. That is how they stick.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Can a word search game really help you learn English?

Yes, when the words mean something to you. A plain word search only trains spotting letters. In Word Search: Learning English every word comes with a picture, its pronunciation and a translation, and it comes back in later levels, so finding it turns into remembering it.

Learn more →
Is Word Search: Learning English good for beginners?

It starts at zero. The first levels use everyday A1 words like CAT, DOG and APPLE, show the full word next to its translation, and use a 6x6 grid with big letters. Difficulty grows level by level up to C1.

Learn more →
Is it an ESL game for adults or for kids?

It is made for teens and adults learning English as a second language. The words are real everyday and advanced vocabulary, the pace is yours and nothing about it feels like a children's worksheet.

Learn more →
How many English words can I learn with the app?

About 2,270 English words across 2000 levels and 20 themed worlds, ordered by CEFR level from A1 (beginner) to C1 (advanced).

Learn more →
Does the app say the words out loud?

Every word has a recorded pronunciation. You hear it when you find the word and you can tap the sticker to hear it again as many times as you want.

Learn more →
Will I remember the words after playing?

That is the point of the design. Words you found return in later levels for review, and from A2 on you only see the translation and the first letter, so you have to recall the English word yourself.

Learn more →
Are the medals and diplomas an official English certificate?

No. Medals, diplomas, badges and the A1 to C1 labels are rewards inside the game that celebrate your progress and show how hard the words get. They are not a test of your English and are not a qualification. The labels follow the CEFR, the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages, only as a guide to difficulty. Word Search: Learning English is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Council of Europe, any exam board or any school. If you need to prove your level for study, work or a visa, take a recognized English exam.

How long should I play each day?

One level takes a couple of minutes. Ten minutes a day, every day, beats a long session once a week, and the daily streak helps you keep the habit.

Learn more →
Why are there pictures instead of definitions?

A picture links the English word straight to the thing, without going through your language first. It is faster to learn and easier to recall.

Learn more →
Is Word Search: Learning English free?

Yes. The app is free with ads, with no account and no subscription. A one-time purchase removes the banner and the ads between levels.
